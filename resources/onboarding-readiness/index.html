The Onboarding Readiness Map

Stop measuring onboarding by what new hires have been shown. Define what they should be able to do, with which support, and how a manager will know.

All resources

Start with three things they'll do.

Choose real tasks or decisions, not topics. “Handle a difficult intake question” is useful. “Understand customer service” isn't.

Build the readiness loop.

Selected task:

Real moment: When and where does this happen?

First practice: What will they attempt without being shown the answer first?

In-work support: What tool, template, example, or prompt will they have?

Manager check: What observable action shows readiness?

Evidence: What will you record, and when?

Measure readiness, not attendance.

Choose at most two business measures (such as handoff errors and time to independent work) and one leading signal (for example, observed correct use of the process). Review the first signs within 14 days of the intervention.

A usable definition of done: A new hire demonstrates the task in a realistic situation, uses the right support, and receives manager feedback. Completing a course can be part of that process; it shouldn't substitute for it.
