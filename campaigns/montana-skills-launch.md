# FISCH campaign: Montana workforce skills (Nov 2026–early 2027)

## Campaign decision
Audience: owners and operations leaders in Montana construction, specialty trades, and manufacturing. Goal: win conversations about specific job skills and repeatable learning systems, not sell vague grant consulting.

Primary CTA: https://fisch.life/montana-skills/
Secondary CTA: email hello@fisch.life with one job skill that needs improvement.

## Verified funding facts (November 6, 2026)
- New Montana Skills Fund: $4.8M federal award announced October 15, 2026; initial sectors construction trades, advanced manufacturing, computing including photonics and quantum. Official program design and employer application rules expected in early 2027. https://news.mt.gov/Governors-Office/Gov-Gianforte-DLI-Secure-4_8-Million-to-Expand-Employer-Led-Workforce-Training
- Current Montana IWT program: eligible employers can apply now for skills-based training beginning before June 30, 2027; competitive and not guaranteed; maximum $2,500 eligible full-time employee/$1,000 part-time; employer approval required before training begins; subscriptions and self-paced online learning excluded. https://wsd.dli.mt.gov/employers/incumbent-worker-training-program/
- Do not mix these funding sources, claim FISCH has grant approval, or suggest a managed LMS subscription is reimbursable. Providers benefiting from IWT grants cannot file their customers' applications.

## LinkedIn / Facebook post 1 (Nov): useful announcement
Montana's new $4.8 million Skills Fund is an interesting signal for builders, manufacturers, and trade employers.

But I wouldn't start by asking, "What training can we buy?"

I'd ask: "What job skill matters most, how do we practice it, and how will we know it improved?"

The state expects to announce application details in early 2027. Meanwhile Montana already has an existing Incumbent Worker Training program with separate eligibility rules.

I put the two programs, their key differences, and a simple readiness worksheet in one place:
https://fisch.life/montana-skills/

I'm based in Helena and spend my days building practical learning systems. If there's a role or process you're trying to make more repeatable, I'd be interested in hearing what breaks.

## Short video 2 (90 seconds)
Opening: "Montana has nearly $4.8 million in new workforce training funding. Great. But here's the problem I would solve *before* applying for a dollar."

Show a blank slide titled "Train the crew"; replace with "What must this worker do independently?" Show one concrete example: "Spot a jobsite handoff issue and escalate it using the approved process."

Then:
1. Name the skill and role.
2. Create one realistic attempt.
3. Put an example/checklist at the point of work.
4. Have a manager observe it.
5. Track one business signal (rework, time to proficiency).

Close: "Training should create capability, not just a completion record. The new Montana fund's rules come in 2027; existing IWT funding has different rules now. I put both links and a printable planner at fisch.life/montana-skills."

## Post 3 (two weeks later): example
Imagine your best crew lead is out for a week.

Can the next person reliably carry out the same handoff?

If not, don't start with a 45-minute course. Start with the real decision, one clear example, a two-minute practice attempt, and a manager checkpoint.

That's the kind of skill-building system I want more Montana businesses to use, regardless of whether funding fits.

Here's a free starting point: https://fisch.life/resources/repeatability-checklist/

## Partnership plan — confirmed contacts (added Nov 6, 2026)

This is a market-entry effort, NOT a promise to win state funding.

1. **DLI Business and Education Engagement Bureau — ask to join the information loop.** The official state directory lists Bureau Chief Mike Williams (MWilliams4@mt.gov, 406-218-7452) and Engagement Team Director John Caldwell (John.Caldwell@mt.gov, 406-885-3012). Ask who owns Skills Fund stakeholder participation and provider guidance; whether independent learning-system builders can present examples; when draft eligibility and rules are expected. Directory: https://directory.mt.gov/govt/state-dir/agency/labor
2. **Montana Contractors Association — establish a relationship with Miranda Griffis** (miranda@mtagc.org, 406-442-4162). Their Nov 6 Built To Lead session addresses the exact shift from technical contributor to supervisor, with scenario practice and a take-home aid; a relevant reference point for offering a short, practical employer workshop. Event: https://members.mtagc.org/eventcalendar/Details/built-to-lead-1883955?sourceTypeId=Website ; strategic context: https://mtagc.org/about-us/mca-clc/
3. **Montana Manufacturing Extension Center — explore subcontracted implementation.** State announcement explicitly lists MMEC as a partner. MMEC already supplies customized technical and Lean training, so FISCH's strongest contribution is instructional design, short practice, manager reinforcement, and evidence systems, not pretending to be the subject-matter expert. Contact through https://www.montana.edu/mmec/about/contact.html . Training offering: https://www.montana.edu/mmec/training/
4. **Montana IWT — ask provider-qualification questions, not reimbursement guarantees.** The existing IWT program has independent criteria including department-approved training providers, qualifying job-skill development, direct live trainer access, and employer submission. Ask iwt@mt.gov (Program Manager Chery Sabol listed on official IWT page) how an independent learning designer can qualify or collaborate with an approved technical trainer. https://wsd.dli.mt.gov/employers/incumbent-worker-training-program/

### What to do next (30 days)
- **Week 1:** Send one concise introduction to DLI, one to MCA, one to MMEC. Ask what skill needs are *not* being met by existing training, not "Can you give me grant money?"
- **Week 2:** Speak with two contractors and one manufacturer; ask for one hard-to-teach job task, how it is currently demonstrated, and what mistake costs them.
- **Week 3:** Create a *fictional* demonstration: a foreperson handoff or technician quality check with a short scenario, decision aid, and observed-performance score. Have an industry SME validate accuracy. No confidential client/employer materials.
- **Week 4:** Offer a short open briefing, “Before You Apply: Five Things an Employer Training Plan Should Prove.” Make it useful whether a company qualifies for funding or not. Capture email interest with clear permission and measure qualified conversations.

### Commercial offer to test
**Workforce Skills System Build** — a fixed-scope project that captures 1–2 pivotal job skills, builds practice + work support, involves an actual technical subject-matter expert, and documents the evidence needed to assess readiness. Optionally sell maintenance and reporting afterward as a separate contract paid by the employer, not necessarily an eligible grant expense.

Do **not** sell a speculative "Skills Fund grant package," resell an LMS under the IWT reimbursement claim, or treat the state fund as a direct payment to FISCH. Employer reimbursement rules for the new fund remain pending.
