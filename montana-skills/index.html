Montana is investing in workforce skills. Is your training ready to deliver?

Two state workforce-training funding opportunities are worth knowing about. They're different programs—with different timelines and requirements.

Discuss a training need ↗Read the program details

1. New: Montana Skills Fund

On October 15, 2026, Montana announced a $4.8 million federal grant to develop the Montana Skills Fund. The first focus areas are construction trades, advanced manufacturing, and computing—including photonics and quantum-related work.

The planned program supports employer-selected training for new and existing workers with outcome-based reimbursement. According to the state's announcement, 80% of approved training costs would be paid after completion and the remaining 20% after six months of continued employment.

Status: Employer eligibility rules, application steps, and program timelines are expected in early 2027. The announcement is not an open application or a promise that any employer or FISCH project qualifies.

Official source: Montana Governor's Office, October 15, 2026 ↗

2. Already open: Incumbent Worker Training (IWT)

Montana's existing IWT program is accepting eligible applications for training beginning before June 30, 2027, subject to available funding and review. It can reimburse up to $2,500 per eligible full-time employee and $1,000 per eligible part-time employee for qualifying new skill development.

It is not funding for generic company-wide training or a subscription library. Self-paced online learning and subscription/membership expenses are excluded. Interactive online training must include trainer access, learning demonstration, and suitable completion evidence.

Employers generally must have operated in Montana for at least one year; participating workers must meet minimum employment and hours requirements. Training must be approved before it starts, and the employer supplies matching funds. Applications should generally be submitted at least 14 days before training begins.

Important: IWT is an employer application, not a FISCH grant. Training providers who directly benefit from the funding cannot submit the application on an employer's behalf, although they may supply documentation and guidance.

What can employers do right now?

Regardless of whether a grant fits, a stronger training plan starts with evidence of the skill you need and the work you want improved.

Workforce training readiness notes

1. Role and job skill: Name a specific new or upgraded capability.

2. Real work: What must the employee do differently?

3. Practice and proof: How will they demonstrate the skill?

4. Business result: What changes if the skill improves?

5. Delivery: Who will train, practice, observe, and document it?

Where FISCH can help

FISCH helps organizations turn expert knowledge and training needs into clear skill practice, usable work support, manager reinforcement, and evidence of improvement. We can design or build eligible-looking components, but the state—not FISCH—determines eligibility and reimbursement.
