# FISCH speaker kit · Workforce skills briefing
November 6, 2026 · Internal preparation, not client-facing

## The offer
**Before you invest in training, prove it can change the work.**

Audience: trade associations, employer cohorts, workforce development groups, small and mid-size businesses.

Format: 20-minute practical presentation + 10-minute discussion, in person (Helena coffee or host facility) or remote.

Public resource: https://fisch.life/briefings/workforce-skills/
Interactive demonstration: https://fisch.life/demos/field-handoff/
Montana source-linked funding explainer: https://fisch.life/montana-skills/

**No fee for the first 1–2 partner learning sessions**, if strategically appropriate and delivery is local/virtual; clarify scope and expenses in advance. This is a marketing choice, not an open-ended promise to every requester.

## Verbatim opening (1 minute)
“Most organizations can tell me what training people have completed. Fewer can tell me what people can reliably do differently afterward.

That's the gap I build around. I'm Patrick Fischer, a learning leader and founder of FISCH in Helena. I work at the intersection of learning strategy, practical content, work supports, and measurable performance.

Today, rather than starting with course design or grant funding, we're going to start where work breaks. We'll take a real-world skill and see what it takes to help someone practice it, use it, and demonstrate readiness.

Montana has announced a new Skills Fund, but application rules are still coming in early 2027. Whether your training eventually qualifies is a state decision. The questions we're about to use will help you design a stronger training investment regardless.”

## Run of show

### Minute 0–5 | The behavior
Ask: “What is one task that your strongest people do reliably, but newer people struggle to repeat?”
Invite 2 fast answers. Translate abstract topics (leadership, communication) into a concrete decision, task, or action.
Show question 1 at /briefings/workforce-skills/.

### Minute 5–12 | Attempt
Open /demos/field-handoff/.
Have audience choose A/B/C silently before discussing. Do not reveal the recommended response before they commit.
Ask: “What details did you need to make the call?” Show feedback.
Ask an attendee to dictate an improved handoff note; submit it.
State: fictional, non-technical decision scenario; validate protocols for a real workplace.

### Minute 12–20 | System, not course
Go back to the five-question briefing and click sections 2–5 as you talk.
1. Which job behavior must change?
2. Where will they practice?
3. What exists at the moment of work?
4. Who reinforces it?
5. What will we observe and measure?

### Minute 20–27 | Group readiness check
Ask audience to check the five boxes for one skill from their business.
Invite one person to share the *first missing element*, rather than every problem.
Ask: “What could you put in place in the next two weeks?”

### Minute 27–30 | Close
“Training can be a course, a workshop, a scenario, a template, or something embedded in the workflow. But it only earns the investment when someone can do the work better.

If you have a skill that keeps getting re-explained, I’d be glad to get coffee, look at an example, or compare notes. My work is at fisch.life. I've also published a free readiness worksheet and the Montana funding overview, with links to the official state information.”

## After-session follow-up
Send the three links and ask “What is the one job skill you'd like your team to demonstrate more reliably?”
For interested respondents, offer a **short, working example of the system** (not a paid diagnostic or a marketed sprint). A paid build can then scope role, task, training support, manager reinforcement, and evidence.

## Proof to collect
- Number of employers attending
- Number who completed the readiness exercise
- Number who named a concrete workforce skill gap
- Follow-up conversations
- Qualified build proposals

Do not infer performance improvement from attendance or checklist scores alone.
